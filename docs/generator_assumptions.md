# Synthetic Data: How It Was Generated and Why

## Why synthetic?
The analysis needs **web-funnel events, device, customer-level order history, product cost and marketing spend** for
the same customers. Public datasets don't combine these. Google's TheLook is itself synthetic and lacks device, checkout
and spend. The GA4/UA samples have no repeat buyers or costs. Olist has no funnel or channel. Rather than pretend, this project
**generates a realistic business from documented assumptions** and labels it synthetic everywhere.

- Company: **Threadline**, a *fictional* Indian D2C apparel brand. Any resemblance to a real company is unintended.
- Window: 1 Jan 2024 – 30 Sep 2026 (snapshot date). Currency: INR.
- Code: `src/generate_data.py`; every parameter is in `src/config.py`. Seeded, so the output is byte-for-byte reproducible.
- Scale: ~820k visitors, 1.14M sessions, ~4M events, 33.5k orders, 20.3k customers.

## How the simulation works (in plain words)
1. **Marketing spend → traffic.** Daily Google/Meta spend divided by a cost-per-click gives clicks. CPC rises as spend rises
   (diminishing returns), and 80% of clicks are first-time visitors. Unpaid channels (organic search, direct, affiliates, referral,
   organic social, newsletter) grow slowly. Everything gets seasonality, weekends and sale events (End-of-Season sales, Diwali).
2. **Visitors browse in sessions.** Each session moves through *product view → add to cart → checkout → purchase*, with
   probabilities that depend on channel, device, whether it's a returning visit, sale periods and a hidden per-visitor
   "intent". A visitor stops being a prospect at their first purchase.
3. **Customers buy again (or don't).** After each order, a customer may silently stop buying. That probability depends on
   acquisition channel and first-order experience (undelivered COD order, returns, bought in a sale, category), and falls with each extra order
   (loyalty). Gaps between orders follow a gamma distribution. Each repeat order needs a successful checkout, so device
   friction affects repeat buying too. Customers also browse without buying.
4. **Orders get realistic economics.** Category mix (seasonal outerwear), prices and unit costs, sale, affiliate and welcome
   discounts, COD vs prepaid payment (by city tier, device, channel), cancellations, **RTO** (undelivered COD), item returns by
   category, shipping fees, and in-flight orders at the snapshot.
5. **The raw files are made imperfect on purpose**, so the cleaning phase is real work: duplicate events, UTC vs IST,
   clock skew, bots, messy UTMs, internal test orders, an ETL duplicate load, an `order_total` bug, untracked sessions
   (ad blockers), and a week of spend exported in USD. See `docs/data_quality_report.md`.

## Calibration to public benchmarks
| Metric | Benchmark (source) | This dataset |
|---|---|---|
| Session conversion, overall | ~2.5% (Smart Insights, via Ringly 2026 stats) | 2.8% all-time, 3.1% last 12 months |
| Conversion, mobile vs desktop | 1.8% vs 3.9% (Smart Insights) | 2.8% vs 4.8% (last 12 months) |
| Cart abandonment | ~70% (Baymard, 50-study average) | 69% |
| Mobile cart abandonment higher than desktop | 80% vs 66% (Baymard via Ringly) | 71% vs 60% (last 12 months) |
| Email converts best, social worst | Opensend via Ringly | Email 7.7%, Paid Social 1.3% |

The benchmarks set plausible ranges. They are not precise targets.

## Ground truth vs what the analysis recovered (method validation)
Because the true parameters are known, we can check whether the methods find them. This is the honest way to present
synthetic-data work: it validates the **methods**, not real-world behaviour.

| Planted in generator | Recovered by analysis | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Mobile checkout→purchase +8 pp from 1 Aug 2025 (desktop unchanged) | Difference-in-differences **+9.6 pp, 95% CI 6.6–12.7** | ✅ True value inside the CI |
| Paid Social dropout probability 0.75 vs Paid Search 0.60 | 12-month repeat rate **16.6% vs 31.2%** | ✅ Direction and size consistent |
| Meta spend ramp ×2.2, CPC elasticity 0.35 (→ CPC ×1.32) | CPC **+33%**, marginal CAC ₹1,250 vs average ₹951 | ✅ |
| Paid Social share of acquisition rises after Mar 2025 | Paid Social share of new customers ~25% → ~32%; blended vs mix-adjusted retention gap appears | ✅ |
| COD RTO 20% (×1.3 in Tier-3), prepaid 2% | **20% / 27% / 2%** | ✅ |
| Sale-acquired customers: dropout +0.10 | 12-month repeat **20% vs 29%** | ✅ |
| Basics first order: dropout −0.08 | Basics repeat rate **+6 pp** vs average | ✅ |
| Tracking loss: 3% mobile / 8% desktop sessions | 4.4% of orders untracked | ✅ (consistent with the device mix) |
| Repeat purchasing is *not* generated by BG/NBD (gamma gaps, covariate-driven dropout) | Plain BG/NBD over-predicts by 48%; MBG/NBD per segment by 13% | ✅ A realistic, misspecified model test |

## What this means for interviews
> "I couldn't find public data combining funnel events, repeat purchases, costs and spend, so I built a documented
> generator calibrated to published benchmarks and planted realistic data problems. Because I knew the ground truth, I could
> show my methods recover it. For example, my diff-in-diff estimate of the checkout redesign was +9.6 pp against a true +8 pp.
> The insights are about the method, not a real company. On real data I'd expect messier signals and would re-validate everything."
